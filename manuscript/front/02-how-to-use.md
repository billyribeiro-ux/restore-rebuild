# How to Use This Book

This book has one rule that matters more than the rest, so it goes first.

**One day at a time. Do not read ahead.**

You are going to want to. You are a person who moves fast and takes on volume, which is part of how you got here, and the temptation to swallow the whole book in one sitting and "get it" will be strong on the very first morning. Resist it. The material is arranged so that each day only makes sense on the foundation of the day before it. More than that: the pace *is* the treatment. You are relearning how to wait, and the book is the first place you will practise.

Thirty days. Thirty mornings. If you miss a day, you do not start over — you simply pick up where you left off the next morning.

---

## Seven practical instructions

1. **Read in the morning, before the market.** Before your phone, if you can manage it. Your first hour trains the rest of the day; that is not a slogan, it is Day 24.

2. **Read the affirmations out loud.** Not in your head. Out loud, in your own voice, where you can hear yourself say them. This will feel ridiculous on Day 1 and it will feel different by Day 14, and Day 14 explains why the difference is not imagination.

3. **Say them even when you do not believe them.** Especially then. You are not lying to yourself; you are rehearsing a sentence until your nervous system stops flinching at it. Belief follows repetition far more often than repetition follows belief.

4. **Keep a notebook beside this book.** Paper, not an app. Several days ask you to write, and two of them ask you to write things you will not want to keep on a device. A cheap notebook is fine. It only has to be yours.

5. **Do the small thing.** Every day ends with one action, and most of them take under ten minutes. They are small because small is what your brain can currently be trusted with, and because thirty completed small things is the evidence file we are building. Skipping them turns this book into reading material. Doing them turns it into training.

6. **Do not trade for size while you are in these thirty days.** If you are flat, stay flat. If you are still in the market, reduce to an amount whose total loss would not change your week. Appendix A gives you the full re-entry protocol and tells you when the size comes back. It does not come back this month.

7. **Read it again if you need to.** Some people need one pass. Some need three. Nobody who needs three is behind.

## What each day looks like

Every chapter has the same shape, and the sameness is on purpose — a fixed structure is easier for a stressed brain to hold, and repetition is how the shape gets under your skin.

- **The Word** — a verse to stand on.
- **Where You Are Today** — what you are probably feeling, named out loud so you stop having to carry it alone.
- **What Is Happening In Your Brain** — one mechanism, in plain language. No jargon left unexplained.
- **What the Word Says To It** — where the scripture and the science meet, and they meet more often than either camp admits.
- **The Meditation** — three to five minutes of stillness, scripted so you can follow it with your eyes closed.
- **Say It Out Loud** — three affirmations, built from that day's verse and that day's mechanism.
- **Today's Small Thing** — one action.
- **Carry This** — one sentence for the rest of the day.

---

## A note on the meditation

If the word *meditation* makes you uneasy, read it as what it is here: sitting still, breathing slowly, and holding one line of scripture in your attention until your body calms down. That is the practice the Psalms describe — *"I meditate on all your works; I consider the work of your hands"* — and it is also, measured in a laboratory, one of the most reliable ways known to lower a stress response. It is not emptying your mind. It is filling it with one true thing instead of forty catastrophic ones.

You do not need a cushion, an app, or a quiet house. You need four minutes and a chair.
