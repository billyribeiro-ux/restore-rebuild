# Your Brain on Blow-Up

## The Word

> Why are you in despair, my soul? Why are you disturbed within me? Hope in God! For I shall still praise him.
>
> — Psalm 42:11

## Where You Are Today

There is one question you cannot stop asking, and it has no answer at three in the morning.

*Why didn't I just close it?*

You knew the level. You had written the level down. You had said the level out loud to somebody. And then the moment came, and you sat there and watched it go through, and you did nothing — or worse, you did the opposite of nothing, and added. And the part that torments you is not the money. It is that you cannot reconstruct the reasoning. You go looking for the trader who made that decision and you cannot find him. There is a hole where his thinking should be.

That hole is the most important thing in this book, so today we are going to look straight into it.

## What Is Happening In Your Brain

You did not have access to your rules. Not *chose to ignore* — did not have access.

Under acute stress, your brain does not degrade evenly, like a car running out of fuel. It **hands control from one system to another**. Two things happen almost at once.

First, stress chemistry floods the prefrontal cortex — the region behind your forehead responsible for holding a plan in mind, weighing consequences, and inhibiting an impulse. Neuroscientist Amy Arnsten's work shows that high levels of stress neurochemicals rapidly impair exactly this region. Not metaphorically. The circuits that hold your trading rules in working memory go offline first, and they go offline fastest.

Second, and more unsettling: as the thinking system fades, control shifts to the **habit system** — older, deeper structures that execute well-learned routines without deliberation. Researchers have shown this experimentally. Stress a person, and their behaviour flips from goal-directed to habitual. They stop acting on what they *want* and start acting on what they have *repeatedly done*.

Now put those two together and read your own blow-up again.

The stress spiked. The rule-holding system went dark. And control passed to the part of you that had rehearsed one particular routine thousands of times — the routine of *staying in and being right*. Every time you have ever held through a drawdown and been vindicated, you were quietly training that habit. It was waiting. And on the day your prefrontal cortex went offline, it took the wheel, and it drove exactly where it had always driven.

This is not an excuse and I am not offering it as one. You are responsible for what happened; Day 8 will hold you to that in detail. But responsibility and explanation are different things, and you have been trying to explain your behaviour with a model — *I knew better and chose wrong* — that is simply not how the machine works. You are hunting for a villain in a room where there was only physiology.

Understanding this changes what the rebuild has to be. If the failure was a lapse of character, the fix would be to want it more. It was not, and wanting it more will not help. The fix is to build rules that execute when the thinking brain is unavailable, and to train the habit system in advance so that what takes over is something you chose. That is Days 15 and 16, and it is buildable.

## What the Word Says To It

David does something remarkable in Psalm 42, and it is easy to miss because it is so plainly written. He talks to himself.

*"Why are you in despair, my soul?"*

He does not describe his despair to us. He turns and addresses it — steps back from the feeling far enough to speak to it, and then tells it where to look: *Hope in God.* This is a man deliberately taking a position outside his own emotional weather in order to give it instruction.

That is exactly the manoeuvre your brain needs when the thinking system is being overwhelmed, and it is not a coincidence that a technique three thousand years old maps onto what the laboratory now recommends. Day 13 will show you the research on speaking to yourself this way, and how measurably it works. For today, notice only this: the man in the psalm is drowning, and he does not pretend otherwise, and he still turns and speaks to his own soul like someone with authority over it.

You have that authority too. You have not been using it. You have been letting the despair narrate.

## The Meditation

Four minutes. Sit upright, feet flat, hands resting open.

Breathe in for four, out for six. Eight times today — longer than usual, because we are practising something specific.

Now, out loud if you can, or inwardly if you cannot, ask yourself David's question in your own words:

*"Why are you so cast down?"*

Do not answer it with the number. Answer it with the honest feeling underneath — *because I am afraid I am finished* — and then say the second half of the verse back to that feeling, slowly:

*"Hope in God. I shall still praise him."*

Do that three times: the question, the honest answer, the anchor. You are not arguing yourself out of anything. You are practising standing beside the feeling rather than inside it.

Finish with one long exhale.

## Say It Out Loud

1. My rules went offline that day, and I am rebuilding a mind that can reach them under pressure.
2. I am not the wreckage of that hour; I am the one now speaking to my own soul.
3. I do not have to feel hope to place my hope in God.

## Today's Small Thing

Write down, in one sentence, the rule you broke. Just the rule, stated as you originally wrote it.

Underneath it, write the time of day it happened, how much sleep you had the night before, and what else was going on in your life that week.

You are not building a defence. You are gathering the conditions. The conditions are data, and from Day 15 onward we are going to use them.

## Carry This

I did not lose because I lacked the will; I lost because I had no plan for the moment my will would not be available.
